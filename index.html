    About Behr تعارف

    Every Urdu ghazal is built on a behr: a meter made of feet (arkān) such as mafā‘īlun and fā‘ilātun, each a fixed pattern of long and short syllables. Poets and listeners feel this structure, but it is rarely seen. Behr makes it visible, audible and computable.

    See

    Each meter is drawn as right-to-left rhythm and as geometry. Every syllable takes its place on a circle, so each behr draws its own signature in the 8-pointed khatam star of Islamic tilework.

    Hear

    A synthesized tabla plays long syllables as a deep dum and short ones as a bright tak, scheduled on the audio clock so sound, light and geometry stay in step.

    Scan

    A scansion engine (taqtī‘) splits a Roman Urdu misra into syllables, weighs them by the rules of ‘arūz, including poetic licence, wasl and zihāf, and finds the meter with dynamic programming.

    Limits and next steps

    • Input is Roman Urdu. Urdu script leaves out short vowels, so scanning it directly needs a lexicon or a model to restore them.
    • Nine meters and their common zihāf forms are modelled so far; classical prosody recognises many more.
    • Tested on misras by Mirza Ghalib and Allama Iqbal. All sample verses are in the public domain.
